mtDNA Kinship Metric

Similarity Score Formula

K(i, j) =
1 V
∑
(VAFi, v − pv)(VAFj, v − pv) pv (1 − pv)

Compares mitochondrial mutations between two individuals. Rare shared mutations receive high weight, while common background variants are downweighted.

K(i, j) – Genetic similarity score
V – Total analyzed mutation sites
VAFi, v – Mutation frequency, individual i
VAFj, v – Mutation frequency, individual j
pv – Background population mutation frequency
i, j – Two compared individuals

Genomic education & scientific primer

Why mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) is superior for lineage tracing

Mitochondrion
Cristae • Circular mtDNA
16,569 bp • 100% maternal
Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) Optimal for ancestry
Location: Mitochondria (cytoplasm)
Copies per cell: 100 – 10,000 copies
Genome size: 16,569 base pairs
Inheritance mode: 100% strict maternal
Recombination / shuffling: Zero (none)
Key advantage: Passed 100% intact from mother to all children without paternal recombination scrambling, creating an unbroken generational record over 200,000 years.
Nuclear DNA Nuclear genome
Location: Cell nucleus
Copies per cell: 2 copies (diploid)
Genome size: ~3.2 billion base pairs
Inheritance mode: 50% maternal + 50% paternal
Recombination / shuffling: Frequent (every generation)
Limitation for lineage: Recombination shuffles alleles each generation, halving ancestral signals every step (e.g. only 3.12% remaining after 5 generations).
